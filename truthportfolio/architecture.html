How the Framework Is Structured

The framework begins with atomic variables, organizes them into six input clusters, derives intermediate scores, and then computes three separate outputs.

A statement is not evaluated through one undifferentiated score. Instead, information about the source, the statement, its delivery, its surrounding situation, the receiver, and the available evidence is kept structurally separate.

Cluster 1
Source Credibility
Bias, expertise, rank relative to the receiver, and stated confidence. These variables are combined into the source credibility term Cred(s).
Cluster 2
Statement Properties
Novelty, internal consistency, falsifiability, and specificity. These become the statement term Stmt(s).
Cluster 3
Delivery Context
Language register, tone, nonverbal presentation, timing, location, and public/private setting. These form Deliv(s,r).
Cluster 4
Situational Context
The surrounding situation, speaker stakes, audience composition, opposing voices, broad stakes, and statement-to-context relevance. The relevance gate determines how much of the surrounding context genuinely belongs to the statement.
Cluster 5
Receiver Properties
Novelty to the receiver, prior knowledge, relationship to the speaker, cognitive or emotional state, and personal consequence. These variables make perceived truth and importance receiver-dependent.
Cluster 6
Corroboration and Evidence
Independent corroborating sources, consistency with established knowledge, and physical or empirical evidence.

From atomic variables to outputs

The atomic variables are not the final scores. The formula engine first combines them into derived quantities such as source credibility, statement quality, delivery, situational context, and receiver response. These values then enter sequential Bayesian updates.

Atomic variables → Derived cluster scores → Bayesian updates → Final outputs

The three outputs

Tabs(s)
The evidence-weighted truth value. It uses the source, statement properties, and available corroborating evidence. It does not depend on the receiver.
Tperc(s,r)
The perceived-truth value for a particular receiver. It begins from Tabs and then incorporates delivery, situational context, and receiver properties.
I(s,r)
The importance value for a particular receiver. Importance is computed separately rather than treating truth as equivalent to consequence.
The central distinction is simple: how well a statement is supported, how believable it appears to a particular person, and how much it matters to that person are three different questions.
