From Inputs to Outputs

The previous page fixed the atomic inputs. Here, those values are passed through the same mathematical structure used by the project's formula engine.

Statement
“The school will be closed tomorrow because a water outage will affect the campus.”
Values displayed on this page are rounded for readability. The engine retains full numerical precision during computation.

1. Source credibility

The four bias variables and four expertise variables are first collapsed into effective bias and expertise values.

\[ B_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{0.98+0.98+0.95+0.80}{4} = 0.9275 \]
\[ C_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{0.05+0.10+0.05+0.05}{4} = 0.0625 \]

These values enter the source-credibility function:

\[ Cred(s) = B_{\mathrm{eff}}^{B_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-0.5}} \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{eff}}} \left(\frac{4D+E}{5}\right) \]
\[ Cred(s) = 0.9275^{\,0.9275^{-0.5}} \sqrt{0.0625} \left( \frac{4(0.50)+0.70}{5} \right) \]
Source-credibility composite: 0.1249

The classmate appears relatively free of obvious bias, but their very low expertise and lack of institutional authority make the overall credibility term weak.

2. Statement properties

Novelty is first balanced so that neither complete triviality nor extreme novelty receives the strongest contribution.

\[ F_{\mathrm{bal}} = 4F(1-F) = 4(0.45)(0.55) = 0.990 \]

The balanced novelty value is then combined with specificity, consistency, and falsifiability:

\[ Stmt(s) = \left( \frac{F_{\mathrm{bal}}+J}{2} \right) G \left( 1-e^{-\ln(100)H} \right) \]
\[ Stmt(s) = \left( \frac{0.990+0.88}{2} \right) (0.96) \left( 1-e^{-\ln(100)(0.98)} \right) \]
Statement composite: 0.8878

This is deliberately much stronger than the source score. The claim is specific, internally coherent, and easy for reality to confirm or disconfirm even though the person reporting it is not authoritative.

3. Delivery

\[ Deliv = W \left( VX^{1.1} \right)^{1.1} \left( \frac{3S+2T+U}{6} \right)^{0.5} \]
\[ Deliv = 0.55 \left( 0.90(0.65^{1.1}) \right)^{1.1} \left( \frac{3(0.95)+2(0.85)+0.50}{6} \right)^{0.5} \]
Raw delivery composite: 0.2668

The implementation uses the normalized form:

\[ Deliv_{\mathrm{final}} = \sqrt{0.2668} = 0.5165 \]

4. Situational context and relevance

Speaker stakes are first adjusted using effective bias:

\[ Z_{\mathrm{eff}} = Z \left[ 1-Z(1-B_{\mathrm{eff}}) \right] \]
\[ Z_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.35 \left[ 1-0.35(1-0.9275) \right] = 0.3411 \]

The relevance gate then determines how much of the surrounding stakes genuinely belongs to this statement.

\[ AD^* = 0.98(0.75)+0.02(0.40) = 0.7430 \]
\[ Y^* = 0.98(0.80)+0.02(0.51) = 0.7942 \]
\[ Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^* = 0.98(0.3411)+0.02(0.60) = 0.3463 \]

These gated quantities enter the situational composite:

\[ Sit^* = AB^{2.1} AA^{0.2} \left( \frac{ 2Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^* +3AD^* +Y^* }{6} \right) \]
Raw situational composite: 0.4554
\[ Sit^*_{\mathrm{final}} = \sqrt{0.4554} = 0.6748 \]

5. Receiver

Receiver novelty begins from \(N=1.10\) and is transformed differently for perceived truth and importance.

\[ N_{\mathrm{norm}} = 1-e^{-1.10} = 0.6671 \]
\[ N_{\mathrm{perc}} = 4.18 N_{\mathrm{norm}}^{0.7} (1-N_{\mathrm{norm}})^{1.5} = 0.6047 \]
\[ N_{\mathrm{imp}} = 1-e^{-N(1+N)} = 0.9007 \]

Receiver term for perceived truth

\[ Recv_{\mathrm{perc}} = \left[ N_{\mathrm{perc}}^{1-P} Q_{\mathrm{rel}} 4R(1-R) \right] AC^{1.75} \]
Raw receiver composite: 0.4534
\[ Recv_{\mathrm{perc,final}} = \sqrt[3]{0.4534} = 0.7683 \]

Receiver term for importance

The same receiver structure is used, but \(N_{\mathrm{imp}}\) replaces \(N_{\mathrm{perc}}\).

Raw importance receiver composite: 0.5213
\[ Recv_{\mathrm{imp,final}} = \sqrt[3]{0.5213} = 0.8048 \]

6. Evidence-weighted truth

The formula engine converts each composite into a likelihood ratio:

\[ \Lambda_c(x) = \exp \left( \frac{x-x_{0,c}} {x_{0,c}(1-x_{0,c})} \right) \]

The cold-start prior is 0.50. The updates then occur sequentially. Because there are no independent corroborating sources, the corroboration step is skipped rather than treated as negative evidence.

8. Importance

Importance begins from its own flat prior of 0.50. The normalized delivery, situation, and receiver composites are first passed through the framework's importance transform.

\[ \widetilde{X}_{\alpha} = 1- \exp \left( 1-(1-X)^{-\alpha} \right) \]
Component Normalized input Transformed input
Delivery 0.5165 0.5455
Situation 0.6748 0.7335
Receiver 0.8048 0.8821

After the contextual updates:

\[ I_{\mathrm{context}}=0.9826 \]

Evidence-weighted truth then enters only through a dampened update:

\[ \Lambda_T = \exp \left[ 0.15 \left( \frac{\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{abs}}}-0.50} {0.50(1-0.50)} \right) \right] = 1.0428 \]
\[ \boxed{ I(s,r)=0.9833 } \]

Why this result is reasonable

The truth value remains relatively low. The statement itself is clear and highly falsifiable, but it comes from a classmate with little relevant authority, has no independent corroboration, and lacks direct evidence that the school has actually decided to close.

The perceived-truth value is much higher. The same statement arrives in a class group, immediately before the school day, during a plausible water disruption, and to a receiver who has an exam the next morning.

The importance value is higher still. Whether the statement ultimately proves true or false, the receiver may need to change travel, preparation, attendance, and exam plans because of it.

Where the framework can still improve

Several atomic inputs remain rubric-guided judgments. Values such as statement-to-context relevance, relationship strength, and audience composition need stronger operational definitions and further calibration.

The framework's neutral points, weights, and transformation parameters are current model choices rather than final empirically established constants. Larger validation studies and independent human or model raters would provide stronger calibration.

A real claim also changes as new evidence arrives. If the school later publishes an official notice, the source and evidence inputs should be updated rather than treating this first computation as permanent.
