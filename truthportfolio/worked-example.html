A Worked Example

Before looking at the formulas, this page shows the exact information supplied to the framework. The final values are not entered manually: they are computed from these atomic inputs.

One of the full worked cases in the theoretical paper is Colin Powell's 2003 UN address on Iraqi weapons of mass destruction. Since that example is already documented in detail, I will not repeat it here. Instead, this demonstration uses a new and much more ordinary statement.
Statement being evaluated
“The school will be closed tomorrow because a water outage will affect the campus.”
Speaker
A classmate posts the statement in the class WhatsApp group and says that they heard it from someone in the administration.
Receiver
Another student in the same class who has an exam scheduled the next morning.
Situation
There are reports of a water interruption in the area, but the school has not issued an official closure announcement.
Corroboration
A second classmate repeats the same claim, but it traces back to the same rumor chain. It therefore does not count as an independent corroborating source.
Each number below is an atomic input or direct rubric-guided judgment. Intermediate quantities such as source credibility, statement quality, delivery, situational context, receiver response, and the three final outputs are calculated later by the formula engine.
Cluster 1
Source Credibility

The classmate appears to have little reason to deliberately mislead the receiver, but has almost no relevant expertise or institutional authority.

Variable Value Meaning Reason
B1 0.98 Political bias No meaningful political incentive is involved.
B2 0.98 Financial bias No meaningful financial incentive is involved.
B3 0.95 Institutional bias The speaker is not acting on behalf of an institution.
B4 0.80 Motivated reasoning The speaker also has reason to hope that tomorrow's school day may be disrupted.
C1 0.05 Formal credentials No administrative or utility-related credentials.
C2 0.10 Peer recognition The classmate is not recognized as an information authority.
C3 0.05 Relevant experience No relevant operational experience.
C4 0.05 Contribution track record No established record of producing school-operational information.
D 0.50 Rank relative to receiver The speaker and receiver are peers.
E 0.70 Stated confidence “Will be closed” is confident wording, although “I heard it from someone” makes the underlying confidence less complete.
In the bias variables, higher values represent the less-biased direction. A value close to 1 therefore indicates little identified bias, not strong bias.
Cluster 2
Statement Properties

These values concern the structure of the claim itself, not whether the classmate is trustworthy and not whether the claim ultimately proves true.

Variable Value Meaning Reason
F 0.45 Novelty relative to field A next-day school closure is meaningful new information, but not an extraordinary type of operational claim.
G 0.96 Internal consistency A water outage affecting campus and causing closure is internally coherent.
H 0.98 Falsifiability The claim can be directly checked the following day and through school or utility information.
J 0.88 Specificity It identifies the event, the time, the cause, and the affected location, although it gives no exact hours or official notice.
Cluster 3
Delivery Context

The message arrives through an informal channel, but at a highly relevant time and in language that is easy for the receiver to act on.

Variable Value Meaning Reason
S 0.95 Language register Direct and fully understandable to classmates.
T 0.85 Tone A direct and somewhat urgent tone fits next-day operational information.
U 0.50 Posture / expression Text-only message, so unavailable nonverbal information is kept neutral.
V 0.90 Time context The message arrives immediately before the school day and exam.
W 0.55 Location / setting A class WhatsApp group has school-related relevance but is not an official channel.
X 0.65 Public vs. private setting The message is visible to a group rather than being purely private, but it is not a public announcement.
Cluster 4
Situational Context

The surrounding situation matters, but the framework also asks whether those stakes actually belong to the statement. Here, they do.

Variable Value Meaning Reason
Y 0.80 General situation A possible water interruption and next-day exam make the situation operationally important.
Z 0.35 Speaker stakes The speaker is affected by the same school day but risks relatively little by repeating the rumor.
AA 0.65 Audience composition Classmates are directly relevant recipients, although they are not operational authorities.
AB 0.90 Opposing voices Little active contradiction is present when the claim is posted.
AD 0.75 Broad stakes Closure affects travel, attendance, exam scheduling, students, and staff.
Rel(s,c) 0.98 Statement-to-context relevance The claim directly concerns the exact school disruption creating the surrounding stakes.
For AB, a high value means little opposition; a low value represents strong opposing voices.
Cluster 5
Receiver Properties

The receiver is not merely an observer. The student has an exam the next morning, making the claim personally consequential.

Variable Value Meaning Reason
N 1.10 Receiver novelty The student expected a normal school day; a closure would require a substantial belief update.
P 0.65 Prior knowledge The student understands normal school routines but has no administrative inside information.
Qrel 0.65 Relationship to speaker A familiar classmate provides some social trust but no special authority.
R 0.65 Cognitive / emotional state The receiver is concerned because of the exam, but not in an extreme emotional state.
AC 0.95 Personal consequence The statement directly affects exam attendance, travel, preparation, and the student's next morning.
Unlike most inputs, N is not restricted to the ordinary 0–1 input scale; it represents the framework's novelty quantity before its separate perceived-truth and importance transformations.
Cluster 6
Corroboration and Evidence

The scenario contains some evidence that a water disruption may exist, but very little evidence that the school has actually decided to close.

Variable Value Meaning Reason
K 0 independent Independent corroborating sources The second classmate traces back to the same rumor chain, so it must not be counted as independent corroboration.
L 0.62 Consistency with established knowledge Closure because of a campus water outage is plausible, but available verified information does not establish that a closure has been ordered.
M 0.42 Physical / empirical evidence There is some evidence of an area water disruption, but no direct documentary or empirical evidence yet confirms the school closure itself.
Cold-start prior: 0.50

There is no ten-statement accuracy history available for this classmate, so the framework begins from its cold-start prior.
